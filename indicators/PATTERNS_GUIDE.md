# Forex & Gold Pattern Guide — What Each Pattern Is and What Usually Happens Next

Companion to the ZTT indicator. Covers the classic chart patterns, candlestick signals,
and Smart Money Concepts (order blocks, FVGs, liquidity, sweeps, POC).

**Read this first:** no pattern "works" on its own. Every pattern below is a *probability*,
not a promise — each one fails regularly. The edge comes from (1) trading them only in the
direction of higher-timeframe structure, (2) at a level that matters (AOI, PDH/PDL, OB, POC),
and (3) with a stop loss and minimum 1:2 reward. A pattern in the middle of nowhere is noise.

---

## 1. Candlestick signals (1–3 candles)

| Pattern | What it looks like | What it usually means / what tends to happen |
|---|---|---|
| **Doji** | Tiny body, wicks both sides | Indecision. Alone = nothing. At a key level after a trend = possible exhaustion, wait for the next candle |
| **Hammer** | Small body on top, long lower wick | Sellers pushed down, buyers slammed it back. At support/after a sweep of lows → bounce is common |
| **Shooting Star** | Small body at bottom, long upper wick | Buyers rejected at highs. At resistance/after a sweep of highs → drop is common |
| **Bullish Engulfing** | Green body completely covers the previous red body | Buyers took control in one candle. Strong at support/OB/FVG, meaningless mid-range |
| **Bearish Engulfing** | Red body covers the previous green body | Sellers took control. Same rule: only matters at a level |
| **Morning Star** | Red candle → tiny candle → strong green closing above the first candle's midpoint | The course's favorite bullish reversal. At an AOI = high-quality long confirmation |
| **Evening Star** | Green → tiny → strong red | Mirror bearish reversal at resistance |
| **Inside Bar** | Candle fully inside the previous candle's range | Compression. The break of the mother bar's high/low often runs — but the first break is frequently a fake (see Sweeps) |
| **Pin Bar** | Same idea as hammer/shooting star | The wick shows where price was *rejected*. Trade away from the wick, at a level |

## 2. Classic chart patterns

### Reversal patterns (appear at the END of a trend, at a major AOI)

| Pattern | Shape | What usually happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Head & Shoulders** | Peak – higher peak – lower peak, one "neckline" under them | INVALID until a candle body closes below the neckline (course rule). After the break, price often retests the neckline from below, then falls roughly the height of the head. Enter on the retest, not the right shoulder |
| **Inverse H&S** | Mirror image at lows | Same logic upward |
| **Double Top (M)** | Two highs at ~the same price | The second high often *sweeps* the first slightly (stop hunt) before dropping. Confirmed on body close below the middle low. Target ≈ pattern height |
| **Double Bottom (W)** | Two lows at ~the same price | Mirror. The second low taking out the first by a little is normal — that's the liquidity grab, not a failure |
| **Triple Top / Bottom** | Three touches | Same as double, more reliable, rarer |
| **Rising Wedge** | Higher highs + higher lows, converging upward | Usually breaks DOWN, especially after a long uptrend. Momentum dying |
| **Falling Wedge** | Lower lows converging downward | Usually breaks UP |

### Continuation patterns (appear in the MIDDLE of a trend)

| Pattern | Shape | What usually happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Bull/Bear Flag** | Sharp move ("pole"), then a small tilted channel against it | Break in the pole's direction, target ≈ pole height. One of the most reliable continuation setups in gold |
| **Pennant** | Pole + tiny symmetric triangle | Same as flag |
| **Ascending Triangle** | Flat top, rising lows | Buyers pressing into a fixed ceiling → usually breaks UP. But expect a sweep of the flat top's stops first |
| **Descending Triangle** | Flat bottom, falling highs | Usually breaks DOWN |
| **Symmetrical Triangle** | Both sides converging | Direction unclear — trade the body-close breakout WITH the higher-timeframe trend only |
| **Range / Rectangle** | Sideways between two levels | Price sweeps one side's stops, then travels to the other side. Asia session builds these every night |
| **Break & Retest** | Level breaks with a body close, price returns to it | The course's #1 continuation entry: old resistance becomes new support (and vice versa) |

## 3. Smart Money Concepts (what the indicator draws)

| Concept | On your chart | Meaning + typical behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **BOS** (Break of Structure) | Small triangles | Body close beyond the last swing point → trend continues that way |
| **CHoCH** (Change of Character) | First BOS *against* the trend | First warning the trend may be reversing. Not an entry — a "pay attention" |
| **Order Block (OB)** | Teal/red "OB" boxes | The last opposite candle before a strong break — where institutions filled orders. Price often returns to the box and bounces from it. Invalid once a body closes through it (box disappears) |
| **Fair Value Gap / Imbalance (FVG)** | Aqua/maroon "FVG" boxes | A 3-candle gap where price moved so fast one side never traded. Price frequently comes back to "fill" the gap before continuing. A filled gap disappears from the chart |
| **Retail liquidity (EQH/EQL)** | Pink dashed "$$$" lines | Equal highs/lows = clusters of stop losses. The market is *drawn* to these levels to take those stops. Expect the level to be swept, not respected |
| **Liquidity sweep (your ✗ SWEEP marks)** | ✗ above/below a candle | **This is your question answered:** a wick pushed past PDH/PDL or a swing point — triggering everyone's stops — but the candle CLOSED back inside. Smart money grabbed the liquidity and rejected the level. Red ✗ above = highs swept (bearish warning). Teal ✗ below = lows swept (bullish warning). A sweep alone is a WARNING, not an entry |
| **Stop hunt / trap (TRAP BUY / TRAP SELL labels)** | Purple labels | Sweep + a reversal candle within 3 bars = the trap is confirmed and the reversal is on. This is the tradeable version of the sweep, with SL beyond the sweep wick |
| **POC** (Point of Control) | Purple line | The price where the most volume traded over the lookback. Acts as a magnet/fair-value: price far above POC = expensive, far below = cheap. Strong support/resistance on the retest |
| **Smart money vs retail liquidity** | — | Retail liquidity = the obvious stops (above equal highs, below trendlines, beyond PDH/PDL). Smart money *uses* it: the move that takes those stops provides the volume for the real move the other way. That's why the obvious breakout so often fails — including a sweep *of* a sweep: a stop hunt on the traders who traded the first stop hunt |

## 4. Session playbook (why sessions matter)

There are **4 major sessions** — the indicator flags each open: **SYDNEY** (5 PM NY),
**TOKYO** (7 PM NY), **LONDON** (3 AM NY), **NEW YORK** (8 AM NY).

The classic daily rhythm on gold and majors:

1. **Asia (Sydney+Tokyo)** builds a quiet range → its high and low become liquidity.
2. **London open** frequently sweeps one side of the Asia range first (the "Judas swing" —
   the fake move), then runs the real direction for the day.
3. **London/NY overlap (Golden Window, ~4:00–6:30 PM UAE)** delivers the biggest expansion.
4. **NY afternoon** dies down — no new entries (course rule: nothing after 10:30 AM NY).

Session strategy that combines everything: mark the Asia range → wait for London/NY to sweep
it (✗ mark) → wait for a TRAP signal or confirmation candle → enter with SL beyond the sweep
wick → TP1 at 1:2, TP2 at 1:4 or the opposite side of the range.

## 5. Priority when signals conflict

The course's own warning applies to this whole document: **information overload loses money.**
If everything is enabled and you try to obey every line, you'll freeze or overtrade. Priority:

1. Higher-timeframe structure (Weekly/Daily/4H) — never trade against it
2. Location — AOI, PDH/PDL, OB, FVG, POC (any respected level; you don't need all five)
3. The trap/confirmation candle — your trigger
4. Pressure %, EMA, session — filters that say "yes, conditions are good"

One clean setup per day per pair, maximum. Skip anything you're not sure about — flat is a position.
